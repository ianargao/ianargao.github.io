Hobby · Technology

Coding

Coding is writing instructions that a computer can follow. Every app, website and game you use started as lines of code.

7 milestones4 key names6 steps

01History

1843

Ada Lovelace writes notes on Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine that include what many people consider the first computer program.

1940s

The first electronic computers are programmed with machine code and cables, one tiny instruction at a time.

1957

FORTRAN, led by John Backus at IBM, becomes one of the first high-level languages, so people can write math in a form closer to English.

1959

COBOL is created for business computing, strongly influenced by Grace Hopper's work.

1972

Dennis Ritchie creates C at Bell Labs. It still sits underneath many operating systems today.

1991

Guido van Rossum releases Python, designed to be readable and friendly. The first website also goes public at CERN.

1995

JavaScript (Brendan Eich at Netscape) and Java (James Gosling at Sun Microsystems) both appear.

02Key people

A
Ada LovelaceWrote the first algorithm meant for a machine.
G
Grace HopperBuilt early compilers and helped make “bug” a famous word after a moth was found in a computer in 1947.
D
Dennis RitchieCreated C and co-created Unix.
G
Guido van RossumCreated Python.

03Get started

01Pick a first language

Python is great for beginners. Use HTML, CSS and JavaScript if you want to build websites.

02Install an editor

VS Code is free and works for almost every language.

03Write Hello, World!

Print your first line and run it. Open the Tools section on my portfolio to see how.

04Build tiny projects

A calculator, a to-do list, a quiz. Small projects teach the most.

05Use Git and GitHub

Save your work, track changes and share it with others.

06Learn from bugs

Every error message is a clue. Read it, fix it, and you get better.

Hobby · Sport

Basketball

Basketball is a fast team sport where two teams try to throw a ball through the other team's hoop.

6 milestones4 key names4 steps8 rules

01History

1891

Dr. James Naismith invents basketball at the YMCA Training School in Springfield, Massachusetts. The first games used a peach basket as the goal.

1891

Naismith writes the first 13 rules of the game.

1932

FIBA, the international basketball federation, is founded.

1936

Men's basketball becomes an Olympic sport at the Berlin Games.

1946

The BAA is founded in the United States. It becomes the NBA after merging with the NBL in 1949.

1975

The Philippine Basketball Association (PBA) starts, the first professional basketball league in Asia.

02Key people

J
James NaismithInventor of basketball.
F
FIBARuns international competitions and the world rules.
N
NBAThe most famous pro league in the world.
P
PBAThe pro league of the Philippines, where basketball is a national passion.

03Get started

01Learn the basics

Dribbling, passing, shooting and defense. Practice each one slowly first.

02Practice shooting form

Eyes on the hoop, elbow under the ball, follow through with a flick of the wrist.

03Play small games

1-on-1 or 3-on-3 helps you learn faster than only running drills.

04Watch and learn

Watching games teaches spacing, teamwork and decision making.

04Rules

  1. Each team has 5 players on the court.
  2. A game has 4 quarters: 10 minutes in FIBA, 12 minutes in the NBA. A tied game goes to overtime.
  3. A normal basket is worth 2 points, a shot from behind the 3-point line is worth 3, and a free throw is worth 1.
  4. You must dribble the ball when you move. Taking too many steps without dribbling is a travel, and stopping and starting again is a double dribble.
  5. A team must shoot within 24 seconds (the shot clock).
  6. Pushing, hitting or holding is a foul. Too many fouls and the player must leave the game (5 in FIBA, 6 in the NBA).
  7. When the ball goes outside the lines, the other team gets it.
  8. The hoop is 10 feet (3.05 meters) above the floor.
Hobby · Entertainment

Anime

Anime is animation made in Japan. It covers every kind of story: action, comedy, romance, sports, and deep stories about life.

6 milestones3 key names4 steps

01History

1910s

The first Japanese animated films appear, short experiments made by early animators.

1960s

Osamu Tezuka, often called the “God of Manga”, helps shape the style with Astro Boy, which started on TV in 1963.

1985

Studio Ghibli is founded by Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata, with producer Toshio Suzuki.

1988

Akira shows the world that anime can be bold and cinematic.

1990s

Series like Dragon Ball and Sailor Moon are watched around the world.

2000s on

Streaming makes anime easy to watch anywhere, and it becomes a global culture.

02Key people

O
Osamu TezukaPioneer of modern manga and TV anime.
H
Hayao MiyazakiDirector of Spirited Away and co-founder of Studio Ghibli.
M
Manga artistsMany anime start as manga, the Japanese comics.

03Get started

01Pick a genre

Shonen (action), slice of life, sports, romance, fantasy, sci-fi. Start with what you already like.

02Choose sub or dub

Sub keeps the Japanese voices with subtitles. Dub is dubbed in your language.

03Use legal streaming

Official platforms support the creators who make the shows.

04Look for the life values

Many anime teach friendship, hard work, never giving up and facing failure. That is why I like them.

Hobby · Design

Creative web design

Creative web design is making websites that look good and feel good to use, with animations, colors and ideas that surprise people.

6 milestones4 key names6 steps

01History

1991

Tim Berners-Lee puts the first website online at CERN. Pages were plain text with links.

1994

Håkon Wium Lie proposes CSS, so style can be separated from content.

1996

CSS1 becomes an official recommendation.

Late 1990s

Flash animations make the web more playful, but need a plugin.

2010

Ethan Marcotte names “responsive web design”: one site that fits every screen.

2016

Figma launches (Dylan Field and Evan Wallace), making design tools run in the browser.

02Key people

T
Tim Berners-LeeInvented the World Wide Web.
H
Håkon Wium LieProposed CSS.
E
Ethan MarcotteIntroduced responsive design.
F
Figma teamMade design collaborative and online.

03Get started

01Start with an idea

Who is the page for? A gift, a portfolio, a card for a teacher. The idea comes first.

02Sketch the layout

On paper or in Figma. Decide where the text and images go.

03Pick colors and fonts

Use two or three colors and one or two fonts. Keep it simple.

04Build with HTML, CSS and JavaScript

HTML for structure, CSS for style, JavaScript for motion.

05Add animation carefully

Small movements feel nice. Too many are distracting.

06Test on a phone

Most people will open it on their phone.

Hobby · Puzzle and sport

Rubik's Cube

The Rubik's Cube is a 3×3×3 twisting puzzle. The goal is to make each of the six faces a single color.

6 milestones3 key names5 steps7 rules

01History

1974

Ernő Rubik, a Hungarian architecture professor in Budapest, invents the cube. He called it the Magic Cube.

1980

The toy is sold worldwide as Rubik's Cube.

1982

The first World Championship is held in Budapest.

2003

The World Cube Association (WCA) is founded to run speedcubing competitions.

2010

Computers prove that any scramble can be solved in 20 moves or fewer.

Today

The best speedcubers solve it in under 4 seconds.

02Key people

E
Ernő RubikInventor of the cube.
W
WCAOrganizes official speedcubing and sets the rules.
S
SpeedcubersPeople who solve the cube as fast as they can.

03Get started

01Learn the notation

R, L, U, D, F, B mean turning the right, left, up, down, front and back faces clockwise. A ' means counter-clockwise, and a 2 means turn twice.

02Solve the first layer

Make a white cross, then place the white corners.

03Solve the middle layer

Move the four edge pieces into place.

04Solve the last layer

Make the yellow cross, place the corners, then orient them.

05Practice

Use the same steps again and again. Speed comes from repetition.

04Rules

  1. The cube has 6 faces. Each face should end up as one solid color.
  2. In WCA competitions, you get 15 seconds to inspect the scrambled cube before solving.
  3. The timer starts when you lift your hands and stops when you touch it again.
  4. You can only turn the cube with your hands. You cannot take it apart.
  5. If the cube is not solved at the end, the result is a DNF (did not finish).
  6. Small misalignments at the end can add a 2-second penalty.
  7. Competitors solve the same scramble, and your average of five solves is often what counts.
Hobby · Mind sport

Chess

Chess is a two-player strategy game on a board of 64 squares. The goal is to checkmate the other player's king.

7 milestones3 key names5 steps8 rules

01History

About 6th century

A game called chaturanga is played in India. It is the ancestor of chess.

Around 600s

The game spreads to Persia as shatranj, and later across the Arab world.

Around 1000

Chess reaches Europe.

Around 1475

The modern rules appear, with the powerful queen and bishop.

1886

Wilhelm Steinitz becomes the first official World Chess Champion.

1924

FIDE, the international chess federation, is founded.

1997

IBM's Deep Blue beats world champion Garry Kasparov in a match.

02Key people

W
Wilhelm SteinitzFirst official World Champion.
F
FIDERuns international chess and the rules.
G
Garry KasparovWorld champion who played Deep Blue.

03Get started

01Set up the board

Each player starts with 16 pieces. The board must have a white square at each player's right-hand corner. The queen goes on her own color.

02Learn how pieces move

Practice each piece one at a time.

03Control the center

Use your pawns and knights to take the middle squares early.

04Develop your pieces

Move knights and bishops out before moving the same piece twice.

05Protect your king

Castle early, then look for your opponent's mistakes.

04Rules

  1. Each player has 1 king, 1 queen, 2 rooks, 2 bishops, 2 knights and 8 pawns. White moves first, then players take turns.
  2. The king moves one square in any direction. The queen moves any number of squares in any direction.
  3. The rook moves in straight lines. The bishop moves diagonally. The knight moves in an L shape and can jump over pieces.
  4. The pawn moves forward one square (two on its first move) and captures diagonally.
  5. A pawn that reaches the other end is promoted, usually into a queen.
  6. Special moves: castling (king and rook move together) and en passant (a special pawn capture).
  7. Check means the king is attacked. Checkmate means it cannot escape, and the game ends.
  8. The game is a draw by stalemate (no legal move but not in check), agreement, or repeating positions.
